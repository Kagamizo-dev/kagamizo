The first risk is that the signal carries no information, which is the hypothesis the backtest is there to reject; a daily decision on a broad index fund from publicly available text has every reason to be noise, and the strategy is built so that noise costs at most the limits allow. The second is model drift: the pinned framework is deterministic in its graph but not in its models' outputs, and a model's replacement or an endpoint's change can shift the decision distribution without any change in the code; the decision files record the model names, and a backtest is tied to them.

The third is the option itself. A cheap, short-dated contract at a modest delta decays quickly, and a right decision on direction can still expire worthless on timing; the rule buys the cheapest fitting contract, which is also the one most exposed to this. Spreads on such contracts are wide, and the preflight's price is the only price the proposal sees. Nothing here is placed by an agent; placing is the owner's step, and only once the stage gate has been passed with the evidence the proofs require.
