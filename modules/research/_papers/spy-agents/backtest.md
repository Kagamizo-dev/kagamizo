The backtest is the framework's own: for each ticker and each trading day of a range, the graph is run as of that day on that day's data and its decision is taken at the next open, with the result reported as annualised alpha over holding the index and as the maximum drawdown. The grid is the tickers and the day range; each day is its own process so a run can be resumed and audited. The record kept names the range, the result, the hit rate of decisions that were right at the option's expiration, and the number of trades, and the strategy moves to paper trading only with a record showing positive alpha and a drawdown within the stage gate's threshold.

Two caveats belong in any such record. The framework's data layer serves news and fundamentals as they are now, so a replay of past days is only as honest as the vendors' point-in-time data, which for free vendors is partial; and a sweep of many days through language models is a spend, capped by the research key's monthly budget, so the range a record covers is bounded by cost, not by choice. Both are stated with the record when there is one.
