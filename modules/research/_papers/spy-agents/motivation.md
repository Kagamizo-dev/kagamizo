Language-model agent frameworks for trading have been published with claims of edge over the index; the one used here, TradingAgents, organises its agents as a firm (analysts, a bull and bear debate, a trader, a risk discussion, a portfolio manager) and ends each run in a decision: buy, sell or hold. Whether such a decision, made once a day on a broad index fund, carries information is an empirical question, and one worth asking carefully because the framework's own reports are its own.

This strategy is the harness for that question. The framework is pinned at a release and run unchanged on open-weight models, so the experiment is reproducible; its decision is expressed as at most one defined-risk option chosen by rule, so the worst case of every trade is its premium and the limits bound the cost of being wrong. The strategy is at the research stage: decisions are made and recorded daily, nothing is placed, and a recorded backtest is what earns the paper stage.
