The backtest replays the rules over contracts that have settled: for each settled event in the category and each day of its listing, the data as it stood on that day (the forecast of record and the observations to date) is fed to the rules, the proposal they would have made is taken at that day's ask, and it is settled at the observation. The grid is the set of cities, question shapes and days replayed; the result is reported as annualised alpha against holding cash at the seed, the maximum drawdown of the running balance under the declared limits, the hit rate of proposals that settled in profit, and the number of trades. A record is kept with the dates it covers and the day it was made, and the strategy may move to paper trading only with a record showing positive alpha and a drawdown within the threshold the stage gate states.

Forecasts of record for past days are the part of this method still to be sourced: the live services serve the forecast as it is now, not as it was, so a replay needs an archive of daily forecasts kept from the day the strategy started reading them. Until that archive covers a settled month, no backtest can honestly be recorded.
