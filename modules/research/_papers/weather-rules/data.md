Two kinds of data enter the rules, both typed into one structure before any rule sees them: forecasts and observations by city and day (a high temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, precipitation and snowfall in inches, each day marked observed or forecast), and, for monthly questions, a per-day climatology for the month (a mean and a standard deviation of precipitation and of snowfall per day). Observations are what a forecast source reports for days already past; they enter as certain.

Forecasts come from one of two free, keyless public services, declared on the strategy: Open-Meteo, which serves both past days and a sixteen-day forecast in the units the rules use, or the National Weather Service's gridpoint forecast, which serves forecast days only (in Celsius and millimetres, converted on the way in). Each city is a fixed coordinate near its reporting station; the set of cities is the set the exchange lists weather on. Climatology is not fetched: it is declared in a data file, and a monthly question that needs a day beyond the forecast horizon without a declared climatology gets no probability at all.

The contracts themselves, their questions and their asks, are read from the brokerage's event-contract listing for the declared category. That read is the only account action the strategy takes before a proposal, and it touches no order.
