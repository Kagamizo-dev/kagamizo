Event contracts settle on an observation: a month's rainfall at a city's reporting station, a day's high temperature. Each pays one dollar if the stated band holds and nothing otherwise, so a contract bought outright can lose no more than its price. That makes them the simplest instrument on which a small, rule-bound strategy can be run and judged: the worst case is known at entry, the resolution is public, and the question is narrow enough that a physical forecast says something about it.

The strategy prices these contracts with deterministic rules and nothing else. No language model is consulted, no discretionary judgement enters; a question the rules do not understand is skipped rather than guessed. The claim under test is modest: that public weather forecasts, turned into a probability with an honest error model, disagree with contract prices often enough and by enough to cover the spread, under limits that cap every loss. The strategy is at the research stage and earns its way to paper trading only with a recorded backtest over settled contracts.
