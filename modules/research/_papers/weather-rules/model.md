A contract's question is parsed from two titles, the event's and the outcome's, into a typed question: a city, a variable (rain or snow over a month, or a day's high temperature), a band with an open or closed end, and a window of days. Three shapes are understood; everything else is skipped.

For a day's high temperature the model is a single normal distribution. Its mean is the forecast high for that day and its standard deviation grows with the lead, the number of days between the day the proposal is made and the day in question: $\sigma_F(\ell) = \min(7,\ 2.5 + 0.75\,\ell)$ degrees Fahrenheit. An observed day has no spread. The exchange's bands are whole degrees, so a band stated as "72 or above" is taken as $T \ge 71.5$, and the probability of a band $[a, b]$ is $\Phi\!\left(\frac{b + 0.5 - \mu}{\sigma}\right) - \Phi\!\left(\frac{a - 0.5 - \mu}{\sigma}\right)$ with the appropriate end dropped when the band is open.

For a month's rain or snow the quantity is a sum over the days of the window. Each observed day contributes its amount with no variance; each forecast day contributes its forecast amount as a mean and a standard deviation of $0.6 \times \text{amount} + 0.03$ inches; each day past the forecast horizon contributes the month's climatological mean and standard deviation per day. The sum is taken to be normal with the summed mean and the root of the summed variances, and the band's probability is read off it as above. Days are treated as independent, which understates the variance of a monthly total when weather is persistent; the limits, not the model, are what bound the cost of that error.

The edge of a contract is the model's probability of the side it pays on, minus its ask. For a YES contract that is $p - \text{ask}$; for a NO contract it is $(1 - p) - \text{ask}$. The declared minimum edge is the threshold below which nothing is proposed, chosen to be well above a typical spread.
